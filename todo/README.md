# Todo

One module of Logseq Plugin (the root README says how the parts fit together; the grammar is in the workspace's value-craft skill, `references/grammar.md`).

Todo is Logseq's own tasks as one short list: what to do today, what is coming up, and what can wait. It lists every task that isn't complete: every status but Done and Canceled (Backlog, Todo, Doing, In Review, and a status of your own). It reads the nodes that have a Status (Logseq's tasks, tagged Task), and the nodes tagged Task or a tag under Task (at any depth) whose Status is still empty, and any node whose Status is there but empty (Logseq keeps its own empty value in it, which was shown as ":logseq.property/empty-placeholder" until 18 November 2026): those are listed as Todo, with Todo's circle, and nothing is written to them until a status is picked. A bullet with no words is left out, with a Status or without, so the list never shows a blank row. It takes a task's day from its Deadline, else its Scheduled. It keeps nothing of its own: every task is a bullet in the graph, and the list is drawn from the graph each time it changes.

## Where it stands

The same list, drawn by the same code, in two places. A change in one shows in the other at once.

- **A page of its own**, in the middle of the window, titled Todo, at most 720 px wide and centred. Open it from the command palette (**Todo**) or with **g** then **d** outside a bullet, as Logseq's own g pairs. The key is set in Logseq's own keyboard shortcuts, where it is listed as Todo; the key sheet (hold Option) lists it among the keys of two letters.
- **Todo in the right sidebar**, a section there only once you open it, narrower and without the title: the checkmark in Logseq's toolbar opens it (the owner, 17 November 2026: "todo button should open up in sidebar"), and so does the command palette's **Todo in the sidebar**. Its heading carries the toolbar's checkmark (Logseq would draw a puzzle piece). Its × closes it; when it was the last thing you opened, the sidebar closes with it.

## What it draws

**The add line** at the top: a + and "Add a todo" on a hairline. Type the task and press Return. A day in the words ("today", "tomorrow", "friday", "by friday", "next friday", "next week", "nov 20", "20 november") is read before you press Return and said faintly at the right of the line ("Friday, Nov 20"); it becomes the task's Deadline and is taken out of the words. A short weekday ("fri") counts only after "on", "by", "due" or "next", so "the sun" stays words. Escape clears what was typed, then leaves the line.

**Find a todo**, under the add line (18 November 2026): Logseq's search glass and "Find a todo" on the same hairline. The list narrows as you type to the tasks that hold every word typed, in their words, the page they are on or a tag, each group with none left out; nothing found says "No task has these words." Return opens the first task found (Shift, in the sidebar), Escape empties the field and a second Escape lets it go. The page and the sidebar each keep their own words.

**The groups**, each under a small grey heading. How the list is grouped is chosen at the top right of the list, on the first heading's line: a small grey word with a chevron, "By day", which opens the library's menu in two parts. Group by: Day, Page, Tag, Priority, Status, a check mark on the one in force. Then by: None, or a second grouping inside each group (Day, Page, Tag, Priority, Status; the first level's own choice faint), so "By day, then page" puts each day's tasks under the pages they are on. Up and Down walk the menu, Return takes a line, Escape or a press elsewhere closes it. The choices are one for the page and the sidebar tab alike, kept in the plugin's settings (and in the settings window, under A page, "Group the Todo list by" and "Then group each group by").

**With a second level**, a group's heading stands in the words' colour and weight, and the groups inside it under the small faint name; inside a group only the groups with tasks stand. What a heading names is not said again on the rows: under a page's heading the rows have no page line, under Today no day.

**Show**, beside the grouping word: "All open ⌄" opens the library's menu with each open status (its own icon, a check mark when it is listed). A press ticks or unticks it in place and the menu stays open, so several can be left out in one go; the last one listed cannot be. When some are left out, the word names what is listed ("Todo, Doing", or "3 statuses") in the words' colour, so a narrowed list never passes for the whole; Show all, at the menu's foot, lists every one again. Rows that go roll up where they stand, rows that come roll out. Kept in the plugin's settings (todoHidden).

- **By day** (the first choice): **Today**, due today or late, the late ones first; a late task says its day in a muted red at the right ("Yesterday", "Nov 10"), one due today says nothing there, the heading says it. **Coming up**, a day after today, soonest first, the day at the right ("Tomorrow", "Mon" within a week, else "Nov 23"). **Any time**, no day, oldest first. Each has the library's nothing-here line when empty ("Nothing to do today.", "Nothing coming up.", "Nothing without a day.").
- **By page**: a group for each page with tasks on it, A to Z, and the journal days together under **Journals** after them. A page's group does not repeat the page under each row; in Journals each row says its day.
- **By tag**: a group for each of the tasks' tags besides Task, A to Z, the heading in the tag's look; a task with two tags stands in both. **No tag** last.
- **By priority**: Urgent, High, Medium, Low, then **No priority**.
- **By status**: Doing, Todo, In Review, then Backlog (what is under way first, what can wait last); a status of your own after In Review.

Outside By day, a group with no task is left out, and the day stands at every row's right ("Today" too, since no heading says it); within every group the tasks are in day order: late first, then by day, those without a day last. With no open task at all, one line says "Nothing to do."

Canceled is left out, and Done stands at the foot. Nothing is coloured but a late day (and Logseq's own colour on a status circle such as In Review's).

**A row**: the task's bullet, the library's, as a task stands on a page (the owner, 20 November 2026: "the todos can have the bullets in fron just like it would normally"), then the task's status circle, Logseq's own, the task's words drawn as Logseq shows them: bold, italic, code, struck and highlighted words as such, a link as its page's name (pressing it opens that page; with Shift, in the right sidebar), a tag as #Name, a block reference as that block's own words (pressing it opens the block), a web address with ↗ after it (opening the browser, as in Find and Threads), and a formula as its letters, under them in grey the page the task is on (a journal's title for a journal day), and the day at the right in grey. The circle is the icon each status has in Logseq (an empty circle for Todo, a half one for Doing, a ticked one for Done by default), so a status given another icon in Logseq shows that icon here; it stands centred on the first line of the words. A row takes no wash under the pointer (the owner, 20 November 2026: "a hover for todos are not necessary"). Pressing the words opens the task, zoomed in to its bullet; with Shift, in the right sidebar. A press just after the last word puts the caret in the words, to change them where they stand (the owner, 19 November: "just like a md link"), Enter or a press away ending it. The page under the words is a link to that page (20 November: "the subtitle pages should be clickable"); with Shift, in the right sidebar.

**Opening a task in place** (20 November 2026; the owner, of the pictured proposal: "good I like all"). A fold arrow stands before a task's circle, Logseq's own, under the pointer; it, or Right on the task's words, opens the task under its row, and Left closes it, as on a row in Find and Relations. What opens is Relations' opened node, not a drawing of Todo's, and inside it Logseq's own editor for the task: its property line (a Deadline with Logseq's calendar), its bullets, typed in as on a page, with Enter, Tab and Logseq's own Undo; then what points at the task. One task stands open at a time. With Relations switched off there is no arrow.

**Setting the status.** Pressing the circle opens the status list under it, as Logseq's own circle does: the library's menu headed Set Status, every status the graph has in Logseq's order (Backlog, Todo, Doing, In Review, Done, Canceled by default), each with its own icon, a check mark on the one in force (none on a Task whose Status is empty). Up and Down walk it, Return takes one, Escape or a press elsewhere closes it. Done turns the circle to Done's and crosses the words out; after a moment the row rolls away (the plugin's one motion) and the task stands in Done. Todo or Doing changes the circle and the row stays. Backlog, In Review or Canceled takes the task out of the list: the row rolls away. The one Undo arrow stands faintly after the words while the row is on screen, and at the bottom right with its words once the row has gone; the Undo brings the row back.

**The right-click menu.** Right-clicking a task (or Shift-F10 on its words) opens the library's menu under its words, each line an icon and a plain name: **Open**, **Open in sidebar**, **Go to** the page it is on; then for an open task **Priority** (Urgent, High, Medium, Low with Logseq's own icons, a check mark on the one it has, and Remove priority when it has one) and **Deadline** (Today, Tomorrow, Next week, meaning next Monday, a check mark on the one it has, and Remove deadline when it has one). A change shows at once; a row that lands in another group rolls up where it was and out where it lands, one that stays only changes in place. The one Undo arrow stands after the words. A Done task's menu has the first three lines only. Status stays on the circle.

**Done**, at the foot whatever the grouping, folded, with a faint count: the tasks done in the last seven days, newest first, crossed out. The fold arrow or the word opens it, rolling out. Its circle opens the same list, so a task there can be set back to Todo or to any status, with its Undo.

**Live.** A task added, ticked, dated or renamed anywhere in Logseq shows here a moment later, through Logseq's change hook; a new day moves Today and Coming up on by itself.

## Its keys

g then d outside a bullet opens the page (set in Logseq's keyboard shortcuts). In the add line: Return adds, Escape clears, then leaves. In the list: Tab reaches the Show word, the grouping word, each circle and each task's words; Right on a task's words opens it in place and Left closes it; Return or Space on a circle opens its status list, Return on the words opens the task, Shift-F10 on the words its right-click menu, Return on Show or the grouping word its menu.

## What it writes

Every write goes through the plugin's one write path (`PluginDoor.write`), and each offers the one Undo.

- **Adding** a task: a bullet with the words at the end of today's journal page (the page is made, by Logseq's own journal call, when the graph has none for today), the Task tag, Status Todo, and the Deadline when a day was read (that day's start, in this Mac's time, as Logseq's date picker writes it). Its Undo takes the bullet away while it is as it was added, and the day's page with it when the add made the page and nothing else is on it; a bullet written in since stays, with a notice.
- **A priority or deadline picked** from the right-click menu: Priority set by its name; Deadline as the day's start in this Mac's time, as Logseq's date picker writes it; Remove takes the property off. Each Undo puts back exactly what was there (none included) while it is as the change left it; changed since, it stays, with a notice.
- **A status picked**: Status set to it. Each Undo puts the status back while it is as the pick left it, and a Status that was empty before is emptied again, not set to Todo; changed since, it stays, with a notice.

Nothing else is written: no tag but Task, nothing outside the graph. Its settings are the grouping (todoGroupBy, todoThenBy) and what Show leaves out (todoHidden); it is switched on and off under Parts in the settings window.
