# Threads

One module of Logseq Plugin (the root README says how the six fit together; `grammar.html` in the workspace draws every rule).

On a page, everything that points at it, named by the property it came through, to follow and dig along. A link made by a property reads from both ends: on the page it points to, each such property is a row under the title and the properties, above the page's own words, with the nodes that hold it, their tag and day. The whole section folds under its own line, What points here, with Logseq's fold arrow, as Linked references does; folded or open is kept for each page, and it stays open while letters are in the find line (8 November 2026, the owner: "allow it to be folded in, the whole thing"). The section is one column (7 November 2026, the owner): a property's name stands on a line of its own with its list under it at the section's full width, and 28px parts one property from the next (the owner picked Tighter, 10 November 2026), so each reads as its own section. Each node is a bullet, drawn with the library's bullet, lk-bul, which is Logseq's own by its numbers and colours in either theme (plugin.js; 7 November 2026, the owner: "treat everything about the bullet to feel like native bullet in logseq"): a ring round the dot while what the node holds is folded, the ring and a larger dot under the pointer, and a press on the dot opens the node as a press on Logseq's bullet opens the block (Shift: the sidebar). On the line: its fold arrow, its dot, then its day in a column of one width, so the words of a list's lines start at one place; a list with no day on any node has no column and its words follow the dot. Each list's heading has one menu for how the list is shown (7 November 2026, the owner: "the grouping and date and other things should be under one menu item"): the heading ends in one faint ⋯ that opens it, after the days and the + (10 November 2026, the owner picked 2 of heads.html); a setting that is not the default is said quietly before the days ("By month · Timeline by Topic"), and nothing is said while nothing is changed. The menu is a short list, one line per setting with its value beside it (Group by, Date, Sort by, Timeline), its choices opening to its side, then Pick nodes, then Move up and Move down, which move the list among the page's lists with the Undo arrow, as a drag does; it is also View in the property name's right-click menu: Group by, then Date: the node's Date, else the journal day it is written on (as always), any date property its nodes hold, Logseq's Deadline and Scheduled among them, or No date, which takes the column away (the owner: "add ability to show or hide date column per section and which date as well"); both kept per property. A date property chosen is what the list goes by: its order (unless another sort is chosen), its months, its year line, its quiet gaps. Each date says what it is of (8 November 2026, the owner: "make it clear what the date is of"): the head names it before its days ("Deadline next on Nov 12", "Date from Aug 20 to Oct 29", "Journal from Sep 3 to Nov 6" for the day a node is written on), and a line dated by something else says its own after its words, where a kind stands: a journal day as the kind it is, Journal in a tag's mono ("I had a good day with Dr. Mei Chen  Journal"), a date property by its name, quiet; the day column keeps one width in every list, so every list in the section stands on one grid (10 November 2026, the audit's 30). A list of one, which has no head, says it on its line; a list inside an opened node, on its first dated line. A task shows Logseq's own circle for its status before its words (8 November 2026, the owner: "threads should be able to show tasks status circles"): its name and icon read from the graph with the rows, as Logseq keeps them on each status, so a status renamed, added or given another icon in Logseq shows so here (9 November 2026, the owner: "have it just stay in sync with whatever icons are chosen or changed"); Logseq's colours, light and dark. A press on it, or Enter or Space once Tab has reached it, opens Logseq's own Set Status list, as a press on Logseq's circle does (9 November 2026, the owner: "can you make the status circle interactive"): every status in Logseq's order, the one in force ticked, and Clear; what is picked is written at once, with the faded Undo arrow. What a node opens hangs from its dot, its own bullets and the nodes that link to it drawn the same way. A node's name carries no underline there until the pointer is on it, except for a wikilink in its words (7 November 2026, the owner: "lines in threads should contain under for the wikilinks"): those words are underlined, faintly, as the library draws a link, and open their page (Shift: the right sidebar), as in a bullet's words and on the hover card; the rest of the name opens the node. The page's own name in a line is underlined too. A page not read yet this session shows its rows and its words together, once the read has answered, so nothing moves after it opens. Rows under a head that names one kind carry no mark, and the find line stands from twenty rows up. The gaps say what belongs together (7 November 2026, the owner: "make the spacing among everything more logical groupings"), the same at every depth: nothing between a head and its list or between the lines of one list; 4px between rows, as between property rows; a line's gap (14px, the grammar's gap between groups) after whatever opened, a list, a node's box or a list inside a node, and between groups: the page's own properties and these rows, a node's properties, what links to it and its bullets, the groups under Group, the nodes since a day, the titles and the lists of two pages side by side. Every line stands 30px, "show all" and a bullet too, and a property's name inside a node wraps between its words, never inside one. A node's name runs to two lines and is then cut, never at a sentence; opened, or while finding, it reads whole. A list's head names three kinds, each with its count beside it, All's too (9 November 2026, the owner: "there should be numbers next to each tab"), drawn as every view of the plugin is: the one in force in the text colour with a short bar under its word, the others quiet, space alone between them (10 November 2026, the audit's 27 and 33, which replace the hairlines of 9 November), then "and 12 more" (a click shows them all); its days stand at the head's right end (the owner: "date range in threads should be right aligned"); a head that wraps starts each line on its words, and the + and Group keep their place on the first line. The fold arrows hide as Logseq's do (7 November 2026, the owner: "arrows should hide too", after asking that morning for them faint on every line): unseen at rest, open or closed, there under the line's pointer; the dot's ring says what is folded. The head folds its list, rolling it up and out as the fold arrow does; in a grouped list each group folds by its arrow, its words do not, and a group that is a tag opens the tag's page. Pointing at a name for a moment raises a card with only what the row does not say, the node's other properties and first lines, and its whole name if the row cut it; with nothing to add there is no card. The card stands to the right of the words beside it, else under the row, or above it where under would cover a list's head or run out of the window. Group offers a property only while its groups would hold two nodes each on the whole, so a Date with a different day on nearly every node is left to Month. Rows fold, group, find, dig into nodes in place, and take a node's value changes with an Undo arrow.

## What it writes

A value changed or cleared in a row, a task's status set from its circle, and a tag added to or taken off a node, each with its Undo. Every Undo of a value, there and on picked nodes, puts it back only while it is as the change left it: a value changed since, by hand or by another plugin, stays, and the Undo says so. Several words or numbers in one property are changed one at a time: each is pressed alone and the box starts from it as written, a link in it whole and a line break kept; empty and Enter takes that one off; the property's name adds another. A bullet added to a node, and a page made from the **+** line, each with its Undo; the line empties on Enter, so Enter twice adds one. A bullet of an opened node is changed where it stands (7 November 2026, the owner: "Allow me to edit bullet in the thread"): a click on its words, or Enter once Tab has reached them, turns them into a box on the same place, as tall as they are and growing with them; the words are saved as they are typed, at each pause, as in Logseq's own bullets (the owner: "you shouldn't need to press enter for text to save"); Enter or a click away ends it, showing the new words at once with no flash of the old ones, Shift-Enter breaks a line, Escape puts back the words it had. The words are as written, a link whole (`[[Page]]`) and a block reference left alone, and go back as Logseq holds a link; emptied, the bullet keeps its words. The faded Undo arrow puts the old words back, unless the bullet has changed since, when it stays and says so. A link in the words opens its page; the bullet's dot opens the bullet (Shift: the right sidebar), as a click anywhere on the line did before. A page named like one in the Recycle brings that page back; a place in the Recycle takes nothing, and says so. The pick bar's **Select all** picks what is shown: what the find line found, of the kind in force, in lists that are open. Its **Delete** deletes the picked nodes or pages: it asks first and names pages apart from bullets ("2 pages with all their bullets and 3 bullets"). A page goes to Logseq's Recycle and the faded arrow brings it back. A bullet, or a tag's page, Logseq deletes for good, so the question then says nothing can bring it back, its yes is the blue one, and the keyboard waits on Cancel: Enter cancels, and only a press of Delete deletes. A dug node shows Logseq's own Deadline, Scheduled, Status and Priority as they are, and a day written into a Date-and-time is kept as a moment, as Logseq keeps it. Undo after clearing words or a number puts them back as they read. The Recycle list under troubleshooting restores a page, or deletes it for good, which no Undo takes back: Restore and a red Delete stand at each line's end, and Delete, and Delete all, first ask under their line, rolled out from it, a bold line saying what goes, then the blue Delete for good and Leave it. The keyboard waits on Leave it, so Enter and Escape both leave it; only a press of Delete for good deletes. A troubleshooting report's Copy is its one filled button. Every write goes through `parts/model.js`, but for those two, which are the Recycle list's own (`parts/diagnostics.js`); both go on through the plugin's one write path (`PluginDoor.write`).

## The look is the shared names'

How the section's pieces look (a tag in mono, the underline a node's name takes under the pointer, the chevron only under the pointer, the tick boxes of a list where picking is on, no counts) comes from the shared class names on what Threads draws, which stand whichever modules run; they are listed in Look's README. With Look switched off the section reads the same, but for the sort's mark before a name, which Look works out.

## What is here

`main.js` is Threads' engine: the parts' three start steps, the hooks they share, the database round and the self-check after a Logseq update; its screen updater runs on the door's one watcher (`plugin.js`) since 5 November 2026, and the engine's names stand under `window.Threads`. `settings.js`, beside `parts/`, says how Threads' settings read in the plugin's one settings window. The rest is in `parts/`:

| Part | What it is | Needed by two-way? |
|---|---|---|
| `shared.js` | the kit: where the library's pieces stand in Threads (outline lines, pop-ups, fields, tick boxes, Undo), the icons; its plumbing (clean-up, warnings, settings, the screen door) is the door's since 10 November 2026 | yes |
| `model.js` | the one place that reads and writes the graph | yes |
| `diagnostics.js` | troubleshooting commands (what the plugin finds on this page, warnings, the tour) | yes (foundation) |
| `two-way.js` | the two-way section: the rows, their drawing, the page's settling, the clicks and the start; its pieces in `two-way/`, loaded after it and meeting it through one bag (`twoWay`): `look.js` (the stylesheets), `group.js` (grouping, folds), `find.js` (the find line, the day and the page it reads out of the words), `peek.js` (the hover card), `dig.js` (a node opened in place, the shift, the trail, writing into a node), `add.js` (the **+** line), `pick.js` (the tick boxes, the bar, Delete), `beside.js` (two pages side by side), `more.js` (Threads Forty's new things on a list) | the feature |
| `proposals.js` | what the **+** line, an opened node and the pick lists propose: Threads' questions put to Predictive's core (its engine, its rules, its one reading of the graph), nothing of its own | optional; the pencilled values in the **+** line and the order of pick lists |

Each optional part can be left out and the module still starts, runs and unloads (`node tools/parts-apart-test.js`). A part that fails to start leaves the rest running, and one notice says which.

## What was removed

The tag and property map (and the tag list's tree), the proposals while a bullet is written (the bullet's own piece, Option-Enter, a page's own properties), search and sidebar looks, page looks, the words by a page's title, a tag's place in the title, PDF quotations, the folded-node note, palette icons, the pickers, link selected text, block commands, and six parts the original kept but never loaded (pages, links, search, inline-props, rich-copy, todos). `git log` has one commit per removal.

**What changes in the section itself:** the row's right-click menu no longer shows **Open in the map**. Its other items stay (**View**, **Reads back as…**, **Hide where it points**), so the two settings the original edits in the tag map (`twoWayNames`, `twoWayOneEnd`) can still be set from the row. The sidebar icon at the end of a line went on 31 October 2026 (Shift-click does the same), with the other changes of that day listed at the top.

## Settings

Two-way: `twoWayRows` (the rows), `twoWayDays` (each node's day after its dot; off, the column goes and the words move left into its place, as they do by themselves when no node of a list has a day to show, on a day's own page usually), `twoWayNames`, `twoWayOneEnd`, `twoWayMentions`, `twoWayMentionsHidden`, `twoWayHideLinkedRefs`, `twoWayHideTagLists`. Others that remain: **Foundation only** under Troubleshooting. **Open troubleshooting** stands there only while Threads runs.
