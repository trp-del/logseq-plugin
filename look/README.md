# Look

One module of Logseq Plugin (the root README says how the six fit together; `grammar.html` in the workspace draws every rule).

How things look, as the visual grammar agreed on 29 October 2026 says. It reads your graph and changes nothing else in it. **What it writes:** only what the × beside a title takes off, a tag from a page, and the same tag put back by the faded Undo arrow the × leaves. On a tag's own page the × reaches every node under the tag, so it opens Ontology's ask on the tag's card, and with Ontology off it is not there.

## What it draws

- **A link** to a page, or to a file in the graph such as a PDF, is the text colour with a faint underline, firmer under the pointer, never blue. A PDF has "PDF" after its name, small and grey, beside the book icon before it. A web address is the same link with a grey ↗ after it. A journal day is quiet grey words; in a property's value, where quiet grey would read as the name's grey, a day keeps the value's colour, no underline, quiet only under the pointer (the calendar icon and the name already say it is a date).
- **A bullet that is a page, or shows one** reads as a link: the text colour with the faint underline. A press on its words opens the page (Predictive).
- **A tag** reads as its name alone, as written, in the serif (New York), upright, in the quiet grey at the words' size (the text colour under the pointer), no #, where Logseq puts it: at the end of a bullet's words and at the right end of a page's title row, the size of any other tag there too. Tags keep one right edge at any width: beside the words while they have 20em, or half the pane in a narrower one such as the right sidebar (the audit's 37), under them on that same edge when the pane is too narrow for that. The × that removes it takes the #'s place under the pointer, so nothing moves.
- **A page's place in the tree**, at the right end of its title row: its tag and the tags that tag sits under, nearest first, "Doctor / Person / Entity" on Dr. Mei Chen; on a tag's own page, only the tags above it. Each name opens its page (Shift: in the right sidebar). Under the pointer an × before a tag the page holds takes it off (Tab reaches it, and Enter or Space presses it), and a faded Undo arrow then waits at the right end of the row, where the tag was, to put it back; in the right sidebar the arrow waits at the end of the page's line of tags, or beside its name when no tag is left. Too long for the row, the line gives way from the middle, then the nearest tag if it alone is too long; the kind stays whole. The same line sits under a page's name in the right sidebar.
- **A page in a property opens it.** A click on a page named in a property's value opens the page, as its underline says (Shift-click: the right sidebar), where Logseq would open its list to set the property. Pointing at the property brings one press after its value, tight against it and centred on its line: under a bullet it stands in the space before the next property, which is the same after every property, so nothing moves, and it stays while the pointer wavers near it; in a page's list it keeps its room: + to add another page where the property holds several, a chevron to set another in its place where it holds one, for a date the date picker. Under a bullet and in a page's list alike. An empty property, words, a number or a choice keep Logseq's click.
- **In a page's list, an icon of what each property holds** before its name: three dots joined in a triangle for a link to nodes, a calendar for a date, letters for words, 123 for a number, a box for a choice. An icon you gave a property stays. Under a node, a property is plain text: no icon in its rows nor in the line of properties under its words, only Logseq's dot and the name. The name is a shade lighter than the value; the value keeps the text colour. No capsules: no edge, no fill, a space between one property and the next, the same after every property; a property holding a pencilled value (Predictive's proposal) counts as filled, so the line does not re-space when the pencil turns solid. Under a bullet the properties run as plain words, and a long line wraps inside its pane, as words do. The property with the keyboard, its value being written or its press reached by Tab, has a thin grey capsule round it, close above and below, taking the press in; a number or words being written take the width of what is typed.
- **A PDF quote's bullet** is an opening quotation mark, “, where the dot would be, centred in the grey circle while the quote is folded: on a highlight, and on a bullet that is nothing but a highlight cited. A note with words of its own beside the citation keeps its dot. With nothing under the quote, Logseq's fold arrow is left out, its room kept.
- **A PDF quote's page** stands after its words, small and grey, as "(p. 2)", where Logseq puts a coloured dot and a bold "P2" before them; a press on it opens the PDF at the quote, as Logseq's did. A quote cited in another bullet's words shows its page after the quote, inside the link.
- **The way to the bullet you write in**, one of three under The outline in the settings: Not shown; Dots and steps (the start): while you write in a bullet, its dot and the dots of the bullets it sits under take the words' colour, and a darker line runs down Logseq's guide line only from each of them to the next, so the way from the top of the page to where you are reads as one thread. Guide lines: the lines that join the bullets are darker instead, each whole, from the outermost bullet in to yours, and the dots stay as Logseq draws them. Either way the rest of the outline stays as Logseq draws it and the words are not changed. In the right sidebar too, and in Threads' opened rows, where the way runs to the row you are at (the one in the soft wash) or to the words you are typing in (12 November 2026, the owner: "the outline doesn't follow the type of outline style of active node"). A switch saved Off before the three choices still reads Not shown.
- **Focusing into a bullet and out**: where Logseq swaps the whole view at once, the bullet you focus into glides from where it stood to the top in about a quarter of a second, its children with it, and the rest of the page fades where it stands. Stepping out runs the same glide back, the page fading in round the bullet, and the bullet comes back selected with Logseq's own selection until the view changes again or you press elsewhere. It is the same for a press on a bullet, Logseq's keys for focusing in and out, a step of the line above a focused bullet, and Back and Forward. A faint up arrow right after the last step of that line steps out one level, as pressing that step does. Cmd-. and Cmd-, go in and out by keys alone: while you write in the bullet, the cursor stays in it and nothing is selected, so Cmd-. goes back in; outside the editor, Cmd-. on the bullet that came back selected focuses into it again, and Cmd-, in a focused bullet steps out one level, as the arrow does. With the Mac's Reduce motion on, the old view only fades. Only the main view moves, not the right sidebar. A switch in the settings, under The outline.
- **The key sheet**: hold Option on its own for a moment (0.7 seconds) and a sheet of keys fills most of the window: Writing, Arranging bullets, Finding, Moving around, and Logseq's keys of two letters (t p, g j), read as each is set now (Logseq's from its own list with your bindings, the plugin's from the settings; a line whose part is switched off is left out). Each line is its words, then each key in a small rounded box in mono, the Mac's marks (⌥T, ⌘⇧K); nothing on it is greyed and the page is not dimmed. While Option is still held it takes nothing: any other key puts it away and does its own job (Option-T read there still chooses a tag), as do a press of the mouse and leaving the window; a quick Option and a letter, as in typing é, brings no sheet; Option with Cmd, Shift or Ctrl never does, nor does it come over a window of the plugin's or Logseq's. Let go once it shows and it stays: letters typed find among its lines, the words found lit, the sheet keeping its size; Up and Down light a line; a key is set there by a press on its box or Enter on its line, then the keys (Backspace sets none, Escape leaves it as it was): a key of the plugin's own (Predictive's five) takes Cmd, Option or Ctrl and is saved at once as the settings window saves it; a key of Logseq's may also be two plain letters one after the other (t y), and is written into the open graph's config (its :shortcuts, every other key there kept), which Logseq takes at once. The keys shown are the ones in force: where the graph's config changes one of Logseq's (⌘P for its commands), the sheet and Find's list of commands show that one. Escape clears what was typed, then closes; so does a press outside it. Logseq's list of commands has it too, as Show the keys. A key of Logseq's own set there is written into the graph's config with every other key kept; when Logseq's keys cannot be read, nothing is written and a notice says so. Find's keys and the focus keys are read from Find and the glide, not written into the sheet. A switch in the settings, under Keys.
- **What a folded node holds**, after its words, small and grey, led by ↳: a circle with the number of unfinished tasks under it, then its pictures, quotes, PDFs and files with how many of each, then up to three of the tags inside, a dot between one and the next as at the title row, no number on them, then the nearest due day, or "was due" once passed. Only while the node is folded.
- Logseq's "+ Add property" under a page's list is a faint word, not a filled button. Logseq's "Add icon" and "Set property" by a title are left out, their room kept. A property with no value is faint wherever it shows. In a page's own list it always shows, so a page shows the properties its tag gives it (the owner, 12 November 2026), its name faint from the first moment the page shows; under a bullet, whether it shows at rest is Predictive's one row, **Hide empty properties until something is proposed**. The pencil after a date is left out. Logseq's two-way list under a node (the nodes of a two-way tag that point at it, in tabs named by their tag) is left out under every node, on any page, a journal day too; a page's own list is Threads' to replace. A property's name that Logseq has cut short shows whole as a tooltip under the pointer.
- **The right sidebar**: the bar on top of it takes the page's own ground with a fine line under it, where Logseq paints it blue grey; the row is tabs (sidebar-tabs.js at the plugin's root; the owner, 12 November 2026): Outline, the page's headings, first on the left; Ontology, the tag or property you are on; Item, the row pressed in a table; Links, with Endnotes under it; then Opened, Logseq's own sidebar with the pages and blocks opened there stacked under their heads; the others show alone, without their heads. Every tab stays in the row (the owner, 14 November 2026): one with nothing to show here (Outline on a page with no headings, Item with no row pressed, Opened with nothing opened) is greyed, a press on it does nothing and pointing at it says why ("No headings on this page"); when the tab picked stops applying, the first tab that applies is picked; the whole sidebar lies on the page's own ground, with a hairline at its edge where Logseq draws a grey bar; the hairline is still the handle that drags the sidebar's width, and darkens under the pointer. Contents, Page graph and Help are left out (the ? at the window's bottom right opens Logseq's help), and so are the "(Dev)" views that developer mode adds. The pages and blocks in it lie flat, with no card, shadow or rounded corner, and a line between one and the next, the first a little way under the bar. A page's name in its item's head reads as a link and opens the page in the main view, where Logseq would fold the item (its arrow still folds it); its properties stand open, with no "Open properties" line: the plugin presses that line once when the item opens and never again for it, so properties you fold stay folded, and a list open in the bullet you are writing (after # or /) is never closed by it.
- **A citation reads as its endnote**: in a bullet on its own page, a raised number stands where "(MRI report, Oct 14, p. 3)" would, the number it has in Endnotes, the same source at the same page keeping its number. Under the pointer it says its source; a press goes there, and a PDF opens at the page; a press on a number in Endnotes goes back to the bullet. The graph keeps the words, and you see them while you edit the bullet, or where it shows away from its page: in Threads, Linked references, Find, an embed or the sidebar. Numbers follow the order on the page. On a page opened before in this session they are drawn with the words, from what was read the last time, and corrected once the page is read again; a page's first visit waits for that reading.
- **Links**, with **Endnotes** under it (one tab since 12 November 2026; a page with no citations has no Endnotes heading), opens a section of the sidebar above its items, as Logseq builds one: it folds by its head and goes by its ×. It rolls out as it opens, what it holds growing on as it is read, and rolls back up as it folds or goes, so the items under it glide; "show all" rolls the rest of a group out. Both follow you from page to page. With one open, the × of the sidebar's last item hides that item and leaves the sidebar open, where Logseq would shut it; a section stays open while the sidebar is shut. **Links** lists the links in the page's words, each once, in two orders, the words at its top: **By kind**, a group for each top-level tag the links sit under (Dr. Mei Chen under Person), the heads by name, then No tag, a few of each then "show all"; or **Order on page**, the one in force with the short bar every view has. With nothing to list, Links and Endnotes say so in the library's one nothing-here line at the top of the tab, as the Ontology does, and Links shows no orders (the audit's 32). A tag, a citation, a cited highlight and a file are not listed. **Endnotes** lists the page's citations, to a node, a web address or plain words, numbered in the order they come, the same source at the same page once, and has no tab on a page with no citations (the owner, 12 November 2026); a press goes to the source, and a PDF opens at that page. A name opens its page, with Shift in the right sidebar.
- **Outline** (named Contents until 12 November 2026), the first tab on the left, lists the page's headings (the bullets started with #, ## or ###) in the order of the page, each one step in from the heading it sits under, so a ### right under a # is one step in, not two. Each is numbered before its words in the quiet grey, 1, 1.1, 1.2, then 2 at the start (the owner, 12 November 2026). A ⋯ at the tab's top right opens its menu: Numbering, as 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, or 1, a, i, or I, A, 1, a, or None, kept with the plugin's settings for every page; and Sections, Fold all and Unfold all. A heading with headings inside it has Logseq's fold arrow before it under the pointer; folded, the headings inside roll up and away and the heading stays. Up and Down walk the menu, Enter takes a line, Escape closes it. The top headings are in the medium weight with a little space above each, the ones inside them in the quiet grey; a long heading wraps under itself, and a link in a heading reads as the name it links to. A press glides the page to the heading; inside a folded bullet, the folds above it open first, as a press on their arrows would; further down a long page that Logseq has not drawn yet, the view steps down until it is there; with Shift the heading opens in the right sidebar. The list follows you from page to page and changes as a # is added or taken off. On a page with no headings the Outline tab is greyed and says so when pointed at.

- **Logseq itself, calmer** (menu icons: each action its own icon, colour only where it says something: red deletes, amber the favourite star, blue the plugin's Find, Ontology and Ontology in the sidebar, the rest grey; every line of Logseq's menus, the ··· menu and a bullet's right-click menu among them, has its icon, the same words the same icon wherever they stand), a switch for each part and a choice of reading width (Narrow, Medium, Wide, Off) under **Logseq itself** in the settings: fewer lines, fills and boxes around your pages, space doing the separating. One typeface, the Mac's own, in place of Inter; a neutral ink in place of Logseq's blue-black, and in dark mode a slightly softer white on a deeper ground. The left sidebar lies on the page's own ground with one fine line at its edge, where Logseq paints a grey slab (lighter than the page in dark mode): its words in the text colour with their icons in the quiet grey (nothing you read is fainter than that), the place you are on a little heavier, no "Navigations" (its edit press still comes under the pointer). The toolbar's icons are drawn a little finer, at Logseq's own strength, and there are two where Logseq has eight: Logseq's ··· menu and the sidebar button. The plugin's places (Find, Ontology, Ontology in the sidebar) head the ··· menu; Logseq's magnifier at the top left opens Find; back and forward are Cmd [ and Cmd ]; Home is left out (Journals heads the left sidebar), and so is the puzzle (Plugins and Plugin settings are in the ··· menu). A page's title is a step smaller (30 px, a little heavier and tighter), its tags and place unchanged. Journal days are parted by space, not a line. Linked references and its like have a medium heading, not a bold one. Scroll bars are thin and faint. Width follows the kind of thing: the page may use the window's width, a table takes all of it, and what is read as lines (the title row, the page's properties, Ontology's lines on a tag's page, Threads' rows, the outline, the references) keeps a reading width, centred (a tag's or a property's page, with the table of its nodes, keeps Logseq's own layout on the left edge of the wide page): 44 em Narrow, 52 Medium (the start), 60 Wide, or Logseq's own with Off. Logseq's tables have no ground on the head, no lines between columns (the column's edge still drags), a hairline under each row and the wash under the pointer. Logseq's search window is the plugin's window: one ground, a hairline, one shadow; no grey bands at the top and the foot, no boxes round icons, no blue ring round the row you are on, which takes the wash; the tip at its foot is left out, its room kept. Logseq's menus, pop-ups and lists (the page's ... menu, a right-click menu, the graph list, a select) are the plugin's own menu: the page's ground, a hairline, a small corner, one shadow, lines at the body's weight, the wash on the line you are on, icons in the faint grey, a hairline between groups. Logseq's windows (Settings, its dialogs) are the plugin's window; in Settings the list of sections lies on the window's ground with a hairline beside it, the one you are on in the wash, and its filled dark buttons (Check for updates, Edit config.edn) take a soft grey ground in place of the black one, so they still read as buttons.
- **A notice**, Logseq's own or the plugin's, is a small capsule of words at the bottom centre of the window, where Logseq draws a large card under the toolbar at the top right; more than one peek above it. Every notice goes after four seconds, a failure too, and stays while you point at it; its × closes it at once.

Every one of these is a switch in the plugin's settings, on by default, applied at once. They are in the plugin's one settings window, under Look (**Plugin settings** in the command palette, or the gear on the plugin's card). Each section is a heading and its rows, nothing between; each row is the thing itself drawn under its switch, a page's name in it heavier than the words, not larger, with the mark beside it sized to match.

## The shared names

There is one short list of class names, one per word of the grammar. They and their stylesheet are the plugin's, in `plugin.js`, with the greys and the property icons, so they stand whichever modules run. A module puts a name on what it draws and carries no look of its own for it; change how a tag looks once, there, and every module follows. Switch Look off and it takes off only what it draws on Logseq's own page (links, tags, capsules, marks, the icons in a page's list, the title row, the sidebar); what the other modules draw keeps its grammar.

| Name | On | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| `lk-row` | a row | the room for its fold arrow |
| `lk-fold` | the row's fold arrow | shown only while the pointer is on its row, or while open |
| `lk-list` | a list that can be picked from | the tick boxes appear when the pointer is on it |
| `lk-tick` | a tick box in such a list | invisible until then; a ticked one stays |
| `lk-node` | a node's name, with its `data-uuid` | the faint underline |
| `lk-url` | a web address, with its `data-open-url` (`PluginDoor.urlHtml`) | the faint underline, a grey ↗ after it; a press opens the browser |
| `lk-bare` | a node's name under a head that names its kind | the plain underline: the head has said what it is |
| `lk-tag` | a tag's name | New York, upright, the quiet grey, the words' size, no # |
| `lk-prop` | a property's name | a shade lighter than the value |
| `lk-day` | a day in the margin | faint, small, figures lined up |
| `lk-count` | a number of things | never shown |
| `lk-pencil` | a pencilled value | mono, grey, a dotted line, no background; a tag inside it keeps its mono and fades once, to the pencil's grey |
| `lk-undo` | the Undo arrow | faded |
| `lk-name` | the small name above a value | faint, small, never pressed |
| `lk-value` | a value, the thing pressed | the text colour; quiet under the pointer; faint when off (`off`) or unwritten |
| `lk-pick` | a value that unfolds | a hairline standing before it; its chevron, drawn by the plugin, faint |
| `lk-alts` | what a value can become | faint words, the one in force (`on`) in the text colour |
| `lk-accent` | the one action of a screen | a press; a key other than Enter faint beside it |
| `lk-close` | the way out, × | faint, the text colour under the pointer |

The buttons have a library of their own: `reference/button-library.html` in the workspace shows every kind, one per job, and the names (`lk-strip`, `lk-btn`, `lk-bar`, `lk-answers`, `lk-lone`, `lk-add`, `lk-more`, `lk-fix`, `lk-x`, `lk-plus`, `lk-views`, `lk-trail`, `lk-head`, `lk-menu`) are drawn in `plugin.js` with the others. `test/library.js` fails on a button drawn by hand.

Text has one scale, as in Logseq: the body is the place's own (16 px on a page, 14 px in a window, a pop-up or the sidebar), `--lk-small` (13 px) is everything secondary (days, counts, notes, keys, trails), `--lk-label` (12 px) the small name over a value, and a tag is the words' own size, its mono's small letters matched to theirs. The greys are measured from the text colour, so a grey inside a grey stays the same grey. `test/library.js` fails on a text size of a module's own.

Threads and Predictive use them; DB Export's toolbar is built on the last six, the Pressing set, settled on 30 October 2026 and drawn in `history/grammar-and-buttons/buttons-mockup.html` in the workspace: a control is a small name over its value, space does the separating, one accent per screen. Look's own settings window follows it.

## How it is built

The root `files.js` lists these in order, each adding to `window.Look`:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `logseq/kit.js` | Logseq's window, Look's base on the door (its stylesheets, settings, listeners and sweep), Look's passes on the door's one watcher of the screen |
| `logseq/db.js` | the one place that reads the graph: every tag with the tag it extends, every page's tags, every property's kind, what a folded node holds |
| `logseq/settings.js` | the switches, in sections |
| `logseq/style.js` | the rules that are only a stylesheet: links, days, tags, property names, capsules, the words by a title, a PDF quote's bullet, and the one pass that puts a quote's page after its words |
| `logseq/calm.js` | Logseq's own screens calmer: the left sidebar, the toolbar, a page's title, the journal days, tables, the search window |
| `logseq/marks.js` | the index of every page's tags and the chains above them, for the parts that group or name pages by their tags (no mark is drawn) |
| `logseq/values.js` | a page in a property opens it; the + or chevron after the value opens Logseq's list |
| `logseq/props.js` | the icon before each property's name in a page's list, and a cut name's tooltip |
| `logseq/title.js` | the tag chain at the title row's right end and in the right sidebar |
| `logseq/folded.js` | the note after a folded node's words |
| `logseq/notices.js` | Logseq's notices and the plugin's alike drawn as a capsule at the bottom of the window, each gone after 4 s |
| `logseq/sidebar.js` | the right sidebar's bar and items, flat on the grammar |
| `logseq/links.js` | the Links (with Endnotes) and Outline sections of the right sidebar |
| `logseq/cites.js` | a citation in a bullet drawn as its endnote's number |
| `logseq/focus.js` | focusing into a bullet and out: the glide, the bullet selected on the way back, the up arrow after the line of steps |
| `logseq/keys.js` | the key sheet while Option is held alone: its lines, each key read as it is set now, and when it comes and goes |
| `main.js` | registers at the plugin's door; starts the parts, passes graph and settings changes on, stops them |

Nothing is kept on Logseq's page when the module stops: its elements carry `lk-own`, its attributes start with `data-lk-`, and both are swept.
