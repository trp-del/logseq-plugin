# Structure

One module of Logseq Plugin (the root README says how the six fit together; `grammar.html` in the workspace draws every rule).

Structure tends the ontology. It has three places, all inside Logseq: a page of the whole, lines on each tag's own page (or in the inspector beside it), and the hierarchy in the list that opens on #. It reads the graph, and writes through Logseq's own calls. Rebuilt on 1 November 2026 from first principles (`structure-first-principles.html` in the workspace).

## What it draws

**Regions, the page of the whole.** Each top-level tag is an area, and its tags stand in it as a small outline, each row with its nodes at the right and a dot where something is suggested. Any tag can be put at the top (Move to… then The top, or dragged to "to the top"), and top-level tags are ordinary tags, in order by name: each can be ticked, dragged, moved under another, merged, renamed or removed when empty. No tag is a kind of its own and none carries a mark: a tag named Entity or Occurrence is a tag like any other. Relationships are drawn between the rows, thin for few nodes and thick for many. A line between neighbouring areas stays in the gap; a line that crosses an area rises to the band above the areas, so no line runs over words. Only the lines of the tag you pressed are drawn. A row's branch rolls out as it opens and back up as it folds, so the rows under it glide, and Every property's groups roll out as they open; a prompt is a picker hung from the thing pressed and moves nothing. Pressing a row lights its lines and shows its relationships and suggestions under the areas. A press on the name opens the tag's page (Shift: the sidebar), Enter too; a press beside the name selects the row, as the arrow keys do. Above the areas stand Regions, Every property and Log, a box that finds a tag, and New top-level tag, which asks for the name and then the first child tags. **Two panes**, beside them, splits the map into two trees side by side, each with its own find box, folds and scroll, so a tag can be dragged from one onto a row of the other when the two are far apart in a long tree; the row under the pointer says where the tag goes ("under Hospital"), "to the top" stands at each pane's foot while a tag is held, and the move waits in the tray like any other. **One pane** puts the areas back. With the right sidebar open the areas scroll sideways.

**A relationship** is written as Logseq stores it, by the property's name: "Conversation  Topic: Cancer  12", the tag that holds the property, the property and the tag it holds, and how many nodes. The number comes from the real values, not from how full the property is.

**A tag's page.** One line in the empty band above the title: Parent (its chevron opens the choice; the parent's name opens the parent's page) and Child tags with Add child tag. The title stays where Logseq puts it; only what opens under the line (a child tag's name being typed, an ask) moves it down, and back. Beside, Relationships and what Structure suggests with its fix stand in the inspector alone, under Parent and Child tags again (the owner, 8 November 2026). The table's columns are the tag's properties, so there is no list of them. Look's place at the title row stands aside, since Parent says it. A journal day never gets these lines, even where the graph holds the day as a tag or a tag shares the day's name; the page is the one drawn on screen, not the one Logseq last named.

**The inspector**, a panel in Logseq's right sidebar (command palette: **Structure: the inspector in the right sidebar**). While it is open and unfolded, a tag's lines stand at its top, all of them (the one line above the title stays), and a property's lines stand there instead of above Logseq's table. Folded, closed, or with the sidebar hidden, a property's lines go back above the table, and a tag's Beside, Relationships and suggestions are not shown. Below the lines it shows the node of the table row last pressed or reached with the arrow keys: its whole words, which the table cuts off (for a page, its name), its tags, every property it holds, and the page it was written on; then what is written in it, the bullets under the node or on the page, as an outline with Logseq's bullets, a folded bullet left folded, the first sixty then Show all (the owner, 8 November 2026: "the inspector should show actual content of page/node below meta data"). Each name opens its page and each bullet its block (Shift: the sidebar); a change made on that page draws it again. While it is shown, a single press on a cell of the table only selects its row (a link in the cell too), and the row carries a quiet wash; a double press does what Logseq's own press did, opening the cell's editor, and the up and down arrows move the selection (the owner, 8 November 2026: "single to select and double to edit"). The tick box, the column heads and a press with Shift, Cmd, Ctrl or Alt stay Logseq's, and with the inspector closed every press is Logseq's. Each part stands under its own head: the tag in its mono, or the property with its icon; below the line, the node's words after its bullet (a page's name after the page icon). The names of the lines are quiet, at the body weight, and each property's name carries the icon Look gives it (Tags a #, On page a page), so what you read is the values. On a narrow sidebar a tag a property is on keeps its count on its line, with Move to, Split and Delete under it. The ··· menu at the top right lists it (with Structure and Find), and it is the first tab of the right sidebar, before Links and Endnotes: picked, it opens and stands alone, without its head. The cube with a + that listed the plugin's panels in a menu is left out, so Structure's tree in the sidebar opens from the command palette. The panel's head shows an i in a circle where Logseq gives every plugin panel the same puzzle, and keeps its fold and its × without Logseq's ··· menu, as Links and Endnotes. The inspector itself only reads: values are changed in the table. With no row pressed it says so in one faint line; on a page that is not a tag's or a property's and holds no table, it says what it waits for.

**The # list.** Logseq's list and keys stay. Each tag gets its path on the empty line above its row; with letters typed, the first tag stands among the tags above and beside it, as rows the arrow keys pass over. Choosing "New tag" then asks where it goes, in the same way: arrows and Enter write Extends, Escape leaves it with no parent, and you are back in the bullet.

**The tray.** It stands on top of Regions and of a tag's page: how many wait, a plain count ("24 links kept, 0 lost"), Old and new, Discard, Apply, and the sentences under them, newest five then "show all". Old and new sets the areas touched as they stand beside what Apply leaves. All four ways of moving end here: Tab, Shift-Tab and drag on a row; ticks and the bar they open; a suggestion's fix; the prompt under the Extends line. A change that can lose nothing is written at once, with the Undo arrow, wherever it is made: putting a property on a tag, node tags, moving a tag or a property, a rename, a new child tag, moving ticked nodes (the owner, 5 November 2026: "I don't want to have to accept every minor change"). What can lose links or values waits for Apply with the plain count: a merge, a removal, a property deleted from a tag, properties merged, a split; and so do changes made together, ticked rows and a new top-level tag with its first child tags. **A change Undo cannot put back whole asks first**, with the blue yes. Apply reads the graph afresh, runs the lines in order and leaves one Undo. That Undo stays at the end of the tray's line until the next Apply: a value filled in or a tag taken off elsewhere no longer takes it away, and it waits on the map only, not at the bottom right of other pages.

**Moving and merging on Regions.** The areas are the choice. The bar says what goes ("Move Hospital and Doctor to: press a tag"), the lines leave, and tags that cannot take it go faint; a press or Enter answers, To the top and Leave it stand on the bar, Escape leaves it. A merge then shows the two tags side by side before it waits in the tray.

**The log.** Every Apply's lines, newest first, with the time and the nodes, kept per graph on this computer.

**A property's page.** Above what Logseq shows: Property type, Node tags (three, then "and 12 more", each with its ×), On tags with how filled and Move to…, Split by its values… and Delete property from tag, Relationships, then what Structure suggests. The title is the rename. **Merge into…**, at the right of Property type, opens the picker with the other properties, and a pick opens the merge's ask there.

**Every property** opens in the place of the areas, in the shape of Logseq's table: one row per property, the tags it is on, what it holds, how filled, sortable. A property on no tag is a row like the others, "No tag" where the tags would be and the nodes that hold it where the fill would be; it comes first in the fill order, since none of its tags fills it, and it opens, renames and goes into a group as any row does; the grammar's row: its fold arrow opens the property's lines in place under it (Property type, Node tags, On tags with Move to… and Delete, Relationships, what is suggested), the name opens the property's page and a tag's name the tag's page (Shift: the sidebar), the tick picks, a press anywhere else selects the row. The tick box and the fold arrow stand in the margin at the table's left, as on a Regions row, so the heads stand over their columns; the sorted head carries Logseq's arrow; a kind word reads in one case (words, a choice, a date, any node). What a press does is said at rest by the grammar's keys: the faint line under the name, mono for a tag, and a faint chevron at the end of On tags (Put on a tag…) and of Holds where the property holds nodes (Node tags…), each opening its choice in place under the row; a kind word Logseq owns and the count carry no key, since nothing changes there. Its right-click menu renames it (the name becomes a field in the row; the rename waits in the tray). **Groups**, for order only: New group at the foot makes one, its name renames it when pressed, its arrow folds it, its × takes it away (what it held goes up a level). A property dragged onto a group's name goes into it, a group dragged onto another goes inside it, onto No group back out. The groups are kept by the plugin on this computer, per graph; nothing is written to the graph and no property changes. **New property**, beside New group, asks which tag it goes on and opens that tag's page: Logseq makes properties, through its own Add tag property there or a name and two colons in a bullet, since a property a plugin makes belongs to the plugin. **Merging and combining properties.** Each row has a tick box, shown under the pointer as on Regions, and the bar above the table says what is picked: Merge into… (one or more) and Combine as… (two or more), waiting faintly until they can. Merging, the rows are the choice ("Merge Physician into: press a property"), the picked ones faint; combining, the bar asks the name of the property they become, and the most held of them is kept under that name, since a property a plugin makes belongs to the plugin. The right-click menu has Merge into… too. Before it waits in the tray, the ask under the kept row shows each property merged away beside the kept one, the tags each is on (on both, moves, new), what becomes of the values when the two hold different kinds ("Words become nodes: the node of that name, made where there is none"), and how many values the kept property cannot take, which stay where they were. Where the kept property takes one value and a node would hold two, the ask says how many nodes and offers only Go to the property, to let it take several values in Logseq first: a plugin cannot change that. At Apply the kept property is declared on every tag the other was on, each node's values move, and the property merged away is left empty and on no tag, kept, to delete from its page in Logseq when you are sure: Logseq deletes a property for good, values and all, and nothing brings it back. So one Undo puts every node's values, the tags' declared properties and any page made for a value back as they were. **A tag's nodes** open from the row's menu, Its nodes: tick them and move them to another tag, or make a child tag from them.

**The prompt**, one drawing wherever a choice is asked for: the chevrons after On tags and Holds in Every property, Add a property… on a Regions row and Move to… in the sidebar (from the menu), the Extends chevron on a tag's page, Merge into…, Specify node tags…, a tag's Move to… and Put on a tag… on a property's page, Move to another tag… over a tag's nodes, and New property. It is a picker hung from the thing pressed, as Logseq's own picker at a cell and the right-click menu (the owner, 5 November 2026: a choice rolling out under a table row, far from the chevron that opened it and pushing the rows down, "doesn't make any sense"): the menu's look, nothing on the page moves, it scrolls with its row, and a press elsewhere, a second press on the same chevron or Escape closes it. Inside: one find line, Logseq's magnifier before it, that reads the ask as its own name ("Put Provider on…", faint until letters are typed, then kept faint at the line's right so the question is not lost; the row it hangs from says what it concerns), then the rows, name and count, stepped in by level, no bullets. A count says what it counts when pointed at: "78 nodes", "on 3 tags", "12 nodes with a value". At rest the tree shows whole levels, the top-level tags always and the next level only when all of it fits in the few, then "show all 23"; letters typed find anywhere in the tree and the matches stand flat, each with its parent's name faint after it. A tag that cannot take the thing stays, faint, and says why, in its title and, under the pointer, where its count stood: the tag itself, beneath it, already under it, the property already on it or inherited from a parent, already a node tag. One row is lit, the one Enter takes; the arrows and the pointer move it, and the arrows reach "show all", after which the first row it brought is lit. A press on a row takes it too. When nothing is found the picker says so, No tag matches “zzz” · Esc clears, and when every row is faint, None of these can take it · point at one to see why. A picker that would run past the bottom of the window opens upward from the thing pressed. Escape clears the typed letters first, then leaves the prompt; Tab leaves it at once.

**The right-click menu** on a tree row or a card line holds what can be done to the row, "Go to this tag" first, ordered by how far they reach with a gap between reaches, and what reaches many says how many nodes before you press (the grammar's menu); it acts on what was clicked and leaves the cursor where it is.

**The right sidebar**: the tree alone, opened on the page's tag, with a cursor and a find box of its own, so you see where a page sits while reading it and can move it from there (drag, Tab, the right-click menu) without moving the main view. A press on a name opens the tag's page, as everywhere; Enter, or Open the map here in the menu, opens the map in the main view on that tag, as a jump Escape comes back from; a change staged from the sidebar opens the main view too, where the tray is. The tree takes the keyboard when it opens or is pressed, and never from a bullet being written. Command palette: **Structure: the tree in the right sidebar**.

## The five rules

None is enforced; each shows as a quiet dot, with its fix a word on the card and in Health.

1. **One place.** A tag under two parents: keep only under one. A tag under no parent is a top-level tag like any other, never an issue.
2. **A child earns its place.** A tag with no nodes and no children: remove. A sibling that declares nothing the other lacks (two or more properties, one of them a relation): merge into it.
3. **A relation has a range.** A property that links to nodes with no tag set as what it points at: set it, guessed from what its values are tagged.
4. **A property lives as high as it is filled.** On three or more siblings: pull up to the parent. Filled by one child only: push down to it. Filled by fewer than a tenth of the nodes (ten or more): drop.
5. **Names say what they are.** A tag whose name reads as a plural: when the singular is a tag, merge into it; otherwise a quiet "may be plural", since spelling cannot tell Notes from Diabetes, and "rename…" opens the name in the tree with the singular guessed, to change or leave. Two tags of one name: merge.

## The keys

No single letter does anything: nothing else in Logseq or in its sibling plugins works that way. A letter typed is Logseq's, so its letter pairs (g j, t w) run on the map as on any page; the find line takes the caret with Cmd-F or a press. What a thing can take is written on its card and in its right-click menu. The keys are the ones every list has.

| Do | Key |
|---|---|
| Move the cursor; open and close a branch; open everything under | `↑` `↓` · `→` `←` · `⇧→` |
| One step in, under the row above; one step out, to the parent's level | Tab, Shift-Tab |
| Tick a row (a node in the nodes list) for a bulk change; the bar above the list then says what the ticked can take, in the place of the three words above the tree (of an empty line under the title in a nodes list), so the rows stay where they were; merging the ticked is on the card | space |
| Open the tag's nodes; from the sidebar, the map on that tag; from a list or a property's card, back to the row's card | Enter |
| In a prompt: the lit row; in an ask under an issue: stage it | Enter |
| Let go, nearest first: the menu, a prompt, an ask, a field, the find text, the ticks; then back the way you came; with nothing left, the keyboard goes back to Logseq, so arrows and Enter are Logseq's again | Escape |
| Apply the tray; discard it. When Undo cannot put it back whole, Apply asks first: Enter says yes, Escape leaves it | Cmd Enter · Cmd Delete |
| Back to the card or list shown before, on out to the page before the map; forward | Cmd-[ · Cmd-] (Logseq's own, and its arrows at the top) |
| Take back the last thing done: a change still waiting, the newest first, then the last Apply, as the arrow | Cmd Z |
| Go to the page of the row, the property on screen or the node in the list; in the sidebar | Cmd O · Cmd Shift O or Shift Enter |
| To the find box | Cmd F |
| Open and fold the row's branch | Cmd ↓ · Cmd ↑ |

Moving a tag is the word on its card, Tab, or a drag of its row onto the new parent's row (the ticked tags together when the dragged one is ticked). Where you came from is kept: a link, a list, a property's card or the nodes is a jump, and Escape walks back along them one step at a time, and the trail line above the card shows the way (a click on a place in it goes back there).

Back and forward are Logseq's own. Every place the map shows is an entry in the window's history (`#/structure?at=…`), so Cmd-[ and Cmd-] and Logseq's arrows go through the cards as they go through pages, each press on the tree a step, the arrow keys down it one step together, and a place comes back with its trail. Logseq's other two-key shortcuts work on the map as anywhere (Cmd K, Cmd comma); the ones in the table are the map's, each doing here what Logseq's does on a page. In a field Cmd Z and the like are the field's. A letter alone is Logseq's, so its letter pairs (g j, t w) run here as anywhere (the owner, 5 November 2026). Shift-Cmd-Z does nothing: an Undo keeps no way forward. Under one of Logseq's windows every key is Logseq's.

The six bulk operations and their sentences: Rename ("Rename Visit to Consultation, 31 nodes"), Merge ("Merge Visit into Consultation, Visit removed"), Split ("Make Follow-up under Consultation with 19 nodes where Kind is follow-up"), Retag ("Move 12 nodes from Conversation to Consultation"), Pull up or push down ("Move Date from Consultation, Procedure and Stay to Medical event"), Add or drop ("Add Place to every tag under Medical event"; "Drop Notes from Stay, 12 values kept on the nodes").

## What Apply writes, and what Undo puts back

Through Logseq's own calls only: a tag's parent (Logseq's Extends), a node's tags, a tag's declared properties, a property's range, a page's name, a tag made or deleted, a node's values of a property. Before each step it asks which graph is open: another graph stops the run where it is, nothing is put back into the graph now open, and the Undo waits in the graph that was written to. If a step fails the run stops and every step made is put back at once: each inverse is run whether or not the one before it worked, and the notice says how many could not be. What could not stays on the arrow ("2 steps not put back"), and the next press runs only that, as a failed Undo does ("31 steps not undone"). The waiting lines stay, naming a tag put back under its new id, so pressing Apply again does the same thing. A merge retags every node, leaving alone a node that already holds the kept tag, moves the children and the declared properties to the kept tag, puts the kept tag in place of the old one in what properties point at, points the values that named the old tag's page at the kept one, looks once more for a node given the tag meanwhile, then deletes the old tag. A removal stops if the tag has nodes by then. Setting what a property points at keeps Logseq's own tags in it (Task, Page), which the map does not show, and Undo puts back exactly what was there. Logseq deletes a tag for good, so Undo makes a **new** tag of the same name in the same place with the same declared properties, child tags and range memberships, and retags its nodes: the same tag to read, under a new id. When another tag holds that name, the two tags of one name that a merge settles, it comes back as the next free name ("Visit 2"), since Logseq allows no rename onto a tag's name. A merge moves the bullets on the old tag's page, with their children, to the end of the kept tag's page before the tag goes, and Undo moves them back onto the tag it makes again ("3 bullets on its page move to Visit"). A removal has no page to move them to: the bullets on its page go. The links to the old tag in bullets become plain words, and Undo does not bring them back, so the sentence says how many, in its ask and in the tray ("12 links become words"). Undo never writes over what came after Apply: a node whose values changed since keeps them, and a tag or page Apply made goes again only while nothing has been written on it, tagged with it or pointed at it; the notice says how many were left as they are. A new tag cannot take a name already in the graph, a tag's, a page's or a property's: Logseq would hand back what holds it, so the line stays open and a notice says so. A rename waits in the tray with the rest, as the design leaves open. A tag dropped on the root row loses its Extends and Logseq puts it under its Root tag. The order of tags among siblings is by name; Cmd-Shift-Up/Down from the design is not built, since Logseq keeps no order among the tags a tag extends.

## Left out on purpose

Groups of properties (order by hand with no meaning in the graph: the card orders by how filled, the table sorts by any column) and Option while dragging to keep a second parent (rule 1 treats a second parent as an issue). A drag moves the tag and everything under it, nothing else, and does exactly what "move under…" does: the row under the pointer shows a line where the tag will go, a row it cannot go under (itself, anything beneath it) takes no drop, and the move waits in the tray.

## Opening it

Structure in Logseq's ··· menu at the top right (with Look's calm toolbar off, also the tree icon in the toolbar, pinned from the puzzle piece's plugin menu), or the command palette: **Structure: the map of tags and properties**. The map is the page `#/structure`.

## The look

The map is drawn into Logseq's own window, so the plugin's shared stylesheet reaches it. The shared names sit on what they name and carry no look of their own here: `lk-row` on a tree row with `lk-fold` on its chevron, `lk-list` on the tree and the nodes list with `lk-tick` on their tick boxes (the shared names say when they show), `lk-node` on a node's name in the nodes list, `lk-pencil` on the name of anything a waiting change touches, `lk-undo` on the Undo arrow. A tag named in words (on a card, in a title row's place, in the table) is `lk-tag`, mono a step smaller; a node's day in the nodes list is `lk-day` in the left margin. The grammar's own elements are drawn as it draws them: a row as a Logseq block with bullet and chevron, the trail line, the pick bar above a list, a prompt as a picker hung from the thing pressed (since 5 November 2026; it rolled out under its row before), group heads as bold words with a gap, the menu with its reaches and counts, the two columns before a merge, the Undo arrow waiting at the right of the tray, keys told as plain words, never chips. A tag's row in the tree is its name in the words font, since the row's words are the tag. A tag named inside words carries Look's `lk-tag`; a long range (Topic points at fifteen tags) shows three and "and 12 more", since a run of fifteen mono names was hard to read (the owner, 30 October 2026). The counts are not `lk-count` (Look hides that), since here they are the point. Property names on the card are not `lk-prop`: that name is for a label beside a value, and here the name is the thing. The rest is the mockup's stylesheet on the grammar's tokens (`../grammar.html`): greys mixed from the text colour, hairlines and space instead of boxes, an icon for what a property holds, italic with a dotted line for what a waiting change touches. The marks, the icons and the Undo arrow are the plugin's one drawing of each. Switch Look off and the map reads the same, a waiting change still pencilled.

## How it is built

The root `files.js` lists these in order, each adding to `window.Structure`:

```
core/         pure: no Logseq, no screen. Runs in Node, so it is tested there.
  model.js      the graph as plain data: tags, the top, parents, declared properties, nodes under a tag, fill counts, what points at a tag
  rules.js      the five rules as issues, each with its fix and the change the fix stages
  changes.js    the staged changes: the sentence, the change shown in the model before Apply, the steps the writer runs
logseq/       the only Logseq-facing code
  db.js         the one reading of the graph (a few datascript rounds) and the one place that writes it
  apply.js      Apply: the steps in order, each inverse kept before its write; the one Undo; a failed batch put back, what failed kept for the arrow
  view.js       the screen: tree, card with its words, tray on top of it, table, Health with the ask, menu, prompts, the trail; the same drawing for the sidebar
  mount.js      the page #/structure and the sidebar panel, hosted by Logseq's React
main.js       the one state every view shares, the commands, the reading, Apply and Undo
```
