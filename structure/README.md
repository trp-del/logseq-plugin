# Ontology

One module of Logseq Plugin (the root README says how the six fit together; the grammar is in the workspace's value-craft skill, `references/grammar.md`).

Ontology (named Structure until 12 November 2026; its folder and the names in its code are still `structure`) tends the ontology. It has three places, all inside Logseq: a page of the whole, lines on each tag's own page (or in the Ontology tab beside it), and, while you write, the hierarchy in the lists Logseq already opens: the list on #, a property's list of values, and the menu on a bullet's tag. It reads the graph, and writes through Logseq's own calls. Rebuilt on 1 November 2026 from first principles (`structure-first-principles.html` in the workspace).

## What it draws

**Regions, the page of the whole.** Each top-level tag is an area, every area the same width (as many beside each other as fit, at least 232 px each, so a name breaks only between its words, and at most 340 px), spread across the page: the first area at its left edge, the last at its right, the room left over in the gaps between them (15 November 2026), and its tags stand in it as a small outline, each row with its nodes at the right and a dot where something is suggested. Any tag can be put at the top (Move to… then The top, or dragged to "to the top"), and top-level tags are ordinary tags, in order by name: each can be ticked, dragged, moved under another, merged, renamed or removed when empty. No tag is a kind of its own and none carries a mark: a tag named Entity or Occurrence is a tag like any other. Relationships are drawn between the rows as hairlines in the quiet grey, in light and dark alike; the counts stand in the list under the areas. The areas share the page's width and go onto a second row when they cannot fit, so none stands past the page's edge and nothing scrolls sideways. A line between neighbouring areas stays in the gap; a line that crosses an area rises to the band above its row of areas (between rows, to the band above the lower row), so no line runs over words. Only the lines of the tag you pressed are drawn. A row's branch rolls out as it opens and back up as it folds, so the rows under it glide, and Every property's groups roll out as they open; a prompt is a picker hung from the thing pressed and moves nothing. Pressing a row lights its lines and shows its relationships and suggestions under the areas. A press on the name opens the tag's page (Shift: the sidebar), Enter too; a press beside the name selects the row, as the arrow keys do. Above the areas stand Regions, Every property and Log, a box that finds a tag, and New top-level tag, which asks for the name and then the first child tags. **Two panes**, beside them, splits the map into two trees side by side, each with its own find box, folds and scroll, so a tag can be dragged from one onto a row of the other when the two are far apart in a long tree; the row under the pointer says where the tag goes ("under Hospital"), "to the top" stands at each pane's foot while a tag is held, and the move waits in the tray like any other. **One pane** puts the areas back. With the right sidebar open the areas share the narrower page.

**A relationship** is written as Logseq stores it, by the property's name: "Conversation  Topic: Cancer  12", the tag that holds the property, the property and the tag it holds, and how many nodes. The number comes from the real values, not from how full the property is.

**A tag's page.** One short line in the empty band above the title: Parent alone (its chevron opens the choice; the parent's name opens the parent's page). The title stays where Logseq puts it; only an ask that opens under the line moves it down, and back. Child tags with Add child tag, Beside, Relationships and what Ontology suggests with its fix stand in the Ontology tab alone (the owner, 8 and 12 November 2026: with several child tags the one line wrapped onto the title), so with the Ontology tab closed they are not shown. The table's columns are the tag's properties, so there is no list of them. Look's place at the title row stands aside, since Parent says it. The page on the right half of two pages side by side gets the same line, and a property's page there its lines. A journal day never gets these lines, even where the graph holds the day as a tag or a tag shares the day's name; the page is the one drawn on screen, not the one Logseq last named.

**The Ontology tab** (the inspector, as it was named until 12 November 2026; in a sidebar narrower than 360 px each label stands over its value, so nothing is cut or laid over another; when another row is pressed, the Item tab keeps the row it showed until Logseq has drawn the new one, so it never stands empty, and the change is one frame: a still picture of the row shown lies over the pane until the next row's view is drawn to the end, its links filled in; the line saying where the row stands, "Consultation · 7 of 56", changes in that frame), a panel in Logseq's right sidebar (command palette: **Ontology: this page’s part, in the right sidebar**). ## The five rules

None is enforced; each shows as a quiet dot, with its fix a word in Suggested, on the tag's page and under the areas. Suggested is closed until asked for: one quiet line, "Suggested 2", opens it, and its heading closes it (the owner, 17 November 2026). Open, it is a plain box with a hairline edge, no green: green is kept for a change about to be made. Each line ends with its fix and a ×, which dismisses that suggestion for good on this graph (kept on this Mac, nothing written to the graph); the faded Undo arrow brings it back. A fix that loses something, such as Remove from Bills, still asks first and waits for Apply.

1. **One place.** A tag under two parents: keep only under one. A tag under no parent is a top-level tag like any other, never an issue.
2. **A child earns its place.** A tag with no nodes and no children: remove. A sibling that declares nothing the other lacks (two or more properties, one of them a relation): merge into it.
3. **A relation has a range.** A property that links to nodes with no tag set as what it points at: set it, guessed from what its values are tagged.
4. **A property lives as high as it is filled.** On three or more siblings, one by one: move it up to the parent ("Date is on Blood draw, Scan and Stay one by one, all under Medical event · Move it up to Medical event"). Filled by one child only: move it to that child ("Only Cancer notes use Diagnosed by, though it is on Phenomena · Move it to Cancer"; until 16 November 2026 "push down to", which the owner could not read). Filled by fewer than a tenth of the nodes (ten or more): drop.
5. **Names say what they are.** A tag whose name is the plural of another tag (Results beside Result): merge into it. A name that only reads plural is not suggested: spelling cannot tell Notes from Diabetes, and the guess read as silly (the owner, 17 November 2026). Two tags of one name: merge.

## The keys

No single letter does anything: nothing else in Logseq or in its sibling plugins works that way. A letter typed is Logseq's, so its letter pairs (g j, t w) run on the map as on any page; the find line takes the caret with Cmd-F or a press. What a thing can take is written on its card and in its right-click menu. The keys are the ones every list has.

| Do | Key |
|---|---|
| Move the cursor; open and close a branch; open everything under | `↑` `↓` · `→` `←` · `⇧→` |
| One step in, under the row above; one step out, to the parent's level | Tab, Shift-Tab |
| Tick a row (a node in the nodes list) for a bulk change; the bar above the list then says what the ticked can take, in the place of the three words above the tree (of an empty line under the title in a nodes list), so the rows stay where they were; merging the ticked is on the card | space |
| Open the tag's page; from the sidebar, the map on that tag; in Every property, the property's page; from a list or a property's card, back to the row's card | Enter |
| In a prompt: the lit row; in an ask under an issue: stage it | Enter |
| Let go, nearest first: the menu, a prompt, an ask, a field, the find text, the ticks; then back the way you came; with nothing left, the keyboard goes back to Logseq, so arrows and Enter are Logseq's again | Escape |
| Apply the tray; discard it. When Undo cannot put it back whole (a tag with nodes deleted for good), Apply asks first and its yes says what goes ("Apply, deleting Message for good"): Enter and Escape leave it, Cmd Enter again says yes | Cmd Enter · Cmd Delete |
| Back to the card or list shown before, on out to the page before the map; forward | Cmd-[ · Cmd-] (Logseq's own, and its arrows at the top) |
| Take back the last thing done: a change still waiting, the newest first, then the last Apply, as the arrow | Cmd Z |
| Go to the page of the row, the property on screen or the node in the list; in the sidebar | Cmd O · Cmd Shift O or Shift Enter |

| Open and fold the row's branch | Cmd ↓ · Cmd ↑ |

Moving a tag is the word on its card, Tab, or a drag of its row onto the new parent's row (the ticked tags together when the dragged one is ticked). Where you came from is kept: a link, a list, a property's card or the nodes is a jump, and Escape walks back along them one step at a time. No trail line is drawn (the owner, 7 and 10 November 2026: one way to open a node in place, with no trail).

Back and forward are Logseq's own. Every place the map shows is an entry in the window's history (`#/structure?at=…`), so Cmd-[ and Cmd-] and Logseq's arrows go through the cards as they go through pages, each press on the tree a step, the arrow keys down it one step together, and a place comes back with its trail. Logseq's other two-key shortcuts work on the map as anywhere (Cmd K, Cmd comma); the ones in the table are the map's, each doing here what Logseq's does on a page. In a field Cmd Z and the like are the field's. A letter alone is Logseq's, so its letter pairs (g j, t w) run here as anywhere (the owner, 5 November 2026). Shift-Cmd-Z does nothing: an Undo keeps no way forward. Under one of Logseq's windows every key is Logseq's.

The six bulk operations and their sentences: Rename ("Rename Visit to Consultation, 31 nodes"), Merge ("Merge Visit into Consultation, Visit removed"), Split ("Make Follow-up under Consultation with 19 nodes where Kind is follow-up"), Retag ("Move 12 nodes from Conversation to Consultation"), Pull up or push down ("Move Date from Consultation, Procedure and Stay to Medical event"), Add or drop ("Add Place to every tag under Medical event"; "Drop Notes from Stay, 12 values kept on the nodes").

## What Apply writes, and what Undo puts back

Through Logseq's own calls only: a tag's parent (Logseq's Extends), a node's tags, a tag's declared properties, a property's range, a page's name, a tag made or deleted, a node's values of a property. Before each step it asks which graph is open: another graph stops the run where it is, nothing is put back into the graph now open, and the Undo waits in the graph that was written to. If a step fails the run stops and every step made is put back at once: each inverse is run whether or not the one before it worked, and the notice says how many could not be. What could not stays on the arrow ("2 steps not put back"), and the next press runs only that, as a failed Undo does ("31 steps not undone"). The waiting lines stay, naming a tag put back under its new id, so pressing Apply again does the same thing. A merge retags every node, leaving alone a node that already holds the kept tag, moves the children and the declared properties to the kept tag, puts the kept tag in place of the old one in what properties point at, points the values that named the old tag's page at the kept one, looks once more for a node given the tag meanwhile, then deletes the old tag. A removal stops if the tag has nodes by then. Setting what a property points at keeps Logseq's own tags in it (Task, Page), which the map does not show, and Undo puts back exactly what was there. Logseq deletes a tag for good, so Undo makes a **new** tag of the same name in the same place with the same declared properties, child tags and range memberships, and retags its nodes: the same tag to read, under a new id. When another tag holds that name, the two tags of one name that a merge settles, it comes back as the next free name ("Visit 2"), since Logseq allows no rename onto a tag's name. A merge moves the bullets on the old tag's page, with their children, to the end of the kept tag's page before the tag goes, and Undo moves them back onto the tag it makes again ("3 bullets on its page move to Visit"). A removal has no page to move them to: the bullets on its page go. The links to the old tag in bullets become plain words, and Undo does not bring them back, so the sentence says how many, in its ask and in the tray ("12 links become words"). Undo never writes over what came after Apply: a node whose values changed since keeps them, and a tag or page Apply made goes again only while nothing has been written on it, tagged with it or pointed at it; the notice says how many were left as they are. A new tag cannot take a name already in the graph, a tag's, a page's or a property's: Logseq would hand back what holds it, so the line stays open and a notice says so. A rename waits in the tray with the rest, as the design leaves open. A tag dropped on the root row loses its Extends and Logseq puts it under its Root tag. The order of tags among siblings is by name; Cmd-Shift-Up/Down from the design is not built, since Logseq keeps no order among the tags a tag extends.

## Left out on purpose

A merge carries the links written in bullets over to the kept tag (16 November 2026): a tag written in a bullet's words (`#Visit`) becomes the kept tag's name, a link (`[[Visit]]`) points at the kept tag, and Undo points both back at the tag put back, leaving alone a bullet whose words changed since. A removal leaves the removed tag's name in those bullets as plain words. Before a merge, a removal, a split or properties merged, Logseq is asked for its own copy of the whole graph, the same one it makes each hour into the graph's backup folder; if it cannot be made, the change still runs and says so.

Before the first write, Apply keeps a record of what it will write and the way back on this Mac, in the plugin's storage folder (`~/.logseq/storages/logseq-plugin/`), and keeps it up to date as each step runs, so the Undo outlives Logseq and works from the Log for 30 days. The way back of each step is recorded before the step writes, and is safe to run whether or not the write happened. A tag or page Apply makes is recorded just after it is made, since its id is known only then. If the record cannot be written, Apply still runs and says that its Undo lasts only until Logseq closes.

Option while dragging to keep a second parent (rule 1 treats a second parent as an issue). A drag moves the tag and everything under it, nothing else, and does exactly what "move under…" does: the row under the pointer shows a line where the tag will go, a row it cannot go under (itself, anything beneath it) takes no drop, and the move waits in the tray.

## Opening it

The tree icon in Logseq's toolbar at the top right, before the ··· menu (with Look's calm toolbar off, Logseq may tuck it into the puzzle piece's plugin menu, from where it can be pinned), or the command palette: **Ontology: the map of tags and properties**. The map is the page `#/structure`.

## The look

The map is drawn into Logseq's own window, so the plugin's shared stylesheet reaches it. The shared names sit on what they name and carry no look of their own here: `lk-row` on a tree row with `lk-fold` on its chevron, `lk-list` on the tree and the nodes list with `lk-tick` on their tick boxes (the shared names say when they show), `lk-node` on a node's name in the nodes list, `lk-coming` on the name of anything a waiting change touches and on what a preview brings, `lk-going` on what a preview takes away (in green text, struck through in red: the dotted pencil is Predictive's alone, the owner, 16 November 2026), `lk-undo` on the Undo arrow. A tag named in words (on a card, in a title row's place, in the table) is `lk-tag`, mono a step smaller; a node's day in the nodes list is `lk-day` in the left margin. The grammar's own elements are drawn as it draws them: a row as a Logseq block with bullet and chevron, the pick bar above a list, a prompt as a picker hung from the thing pressed (since 5 November 2026; it rolled out under its row before), group heads as bold words with a gap, the menu with its reaches and counts, the two columns before a merge, the Undo arrow waiting at the right of the tray, keys told as plain words, never chips. A tag's row in the tree is its name in the words font, since the row's words are the tag. A tag named inside words carries Look's `lk-tag`; a long range (Topic points at fifteen tags) shows three and "and 12 more", since a run of fifteen mono names was hard to read (the owner, 30 October 2026). The counts are not `lk-count` (Look hides that), since here they are the point. Property names in the Ontology tab's lines are not `lk-prop`: that name is for a label beside a value, and here the name is the thing. The rest is the mockup's stylesheet on the grammar's tokens (the `--lk-*` names in `plugin.js`): greys mixed from the text colour, hairlines and space instead of boxes, an icon for what a property holds, italic with a dotted line for what a waiting change touches. The marks, the icons and the Undo arrow are the plugin's one drawing of each. Switch Look off and the map reads the same, a waiting change still in green text.

## How it is built

The root `files.js` lists these in order, each adding to `window.Structure`:

```
core/         pure: no Logseq, no screen. Runs in Node, so it is tested there.
  model.js      the graph as plain data: tags, the top, parents, declared properties, nodes under a tag, fill counts, what points at a tag
  rules.js      the five rules as issues, each with its fix and the change the fix stages
  values.js     a property's values moved into another property: which the kept property can take, in what form, and what a node would hold
  changes.js    the staged changes: the sentence, the change shown in the model before Apply, the steps the writer runs
logseq/       the only Logseq-facing code
  db.js         the one reading of the graph (a few datascript rounds) and the one place that writes it
  records.js    the record of every Apply on this Mac (the plugin's storage folder): what was written and the way back, as data, kept 30 days
  apply.js      Apply: the steps in order, each inverse kept before its write; the one Undo; a failed batch put back, what failed kept for the arrow
  view.js       the screen: Regions, Every property, the tray on top of it, a tag's line and its lines in the Ontology tab, the table, the ask, menu, prompts
  regions.js    the Regions page: each top-level tag an area, the relationships drawn as lines between the rows
  inspector.js  the Ontology tab and the Item tab of Logseq's right sidebar
  hash.js       the hierarchy in the list that opens on #: your tags under your top-level tags before any letter, each tag's path after its name, the tags under the lit tag, a near name
  value-list.js a property's list of values, each tag's pages under its name; Logseq's list of tags as a tree
  where.js      a new tag asks where it goes: the whole tree, folds, a find line, Undo
  tag-menu.js   a bullet's tag, right-clicked: the tags around it, to change to
  mount.js      the page #/structure and the Ontology tab in the right sidebar, hosted by Logseq's React
main.js       the one state every view shares, the commands, the reading, Apply and Undo
```
